# Goal A: Strategic Outcomes by Psychological State

## The Ultimate Goal
The single ultimate goal is to get qualified candidates to apply for roles where they are a strong mutual fit. However, pursuing this ultimate goal aggressively in every conversation is counterproductive. The ideal outcome is not always the ideal move. My specific tactical goal must adapt dynamically to the psychological state of the candidate. This document defines which outcome to prioritize based on the candidate's real-time reception and mood, when to push for the ideal outcome, when to settle for a minimum viable outcome, and when to exit gracefully.

---

## State 1: High Interest and Genuine Curiosity (The "Tell Me More" State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate is warm, engaged, and asking specific clarifying questions about the role, the team, or the tech stack. They have clearly read the initial message and are actively evaluating themselves against the opportunity. The psychological door is wide open.

### The Ideal Goal
Secure a concrete commitment to the next step, which is typically sharing the direct interview link or scheduling a conversation with the hiring manager. I share the direct interview link immediately and plant the seed that they should come back to me after completing it so I can get them onboarded on my end.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they are interested but hesitant to commit to the interview immediately, I lower the bar by offering to send the job description link for review first, with the understanding that they can decide after reviewing it. This keeps the conversation alive without pressure.

### The Exit Strategy
If they express interest but are entirely non-committal and do not engage with either the interview link or the job description, I ask if they would prefer I reach out at a later time. If they say no or remain non-committal, I thank them and move on.

---

## State 2: Skeptical but Asking Detailed Questions (The "Prove It" State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate is engaged but guarded. They are throwing objections at me—salary, remote policy, team structure—but they are asking these questions because they are actually interested and are stress-testing the opportunity against their personal dealbreakers.

### The Ideal Goal
Build trust and credibility by answering every question with grounded data from the knowledge base, demonstrating complete transparency. The intermediate goal is to shift them from "skeptical" to "curious" by proving that the opportunity is legitimate and well-researched. Once they soften, I pivot to sharing the direct interview link.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If I cannot fully resolve their skepticism in one exchange, I offer to share my LinkedIn profile or the Navii company page to build credibility. I also offer to send the job description link so they can validate the details themselves. This creates a low-pressure path for them to continue the conversation at their own pace.

### The Exit Strategy
If they remain deeply skeptical after multiple exchanges and do not engage with any of the credibility-building links, I acknowledge their concerns, thank them for their time, and ask if they know anyone in their network who might be a better fit. I exit gracefully without burning the relationship.

---

## State 3: Overwhelmed, Busy, or Low-Energy (The "Not Right Now" State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate is not rejecting the opportunity itself; they are rejecting the time commitment they assume the opportunity will require. They may have said "I'm busy" or "the timing is bad."

### The Ideal Goal
Drastically lower the friction bar. I abandon the ideal outcome of an immediate interview or application and instead aim for the lowest possible commitment: a ten-minute exploratory phone call or a quick exchange of questions via WhatsApp. If they agree to even that, I have successfully unlocked the door, and I can slowly rebuild momentum from there.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they cannot commit to even a short call, I ask for permission to share the job description link so they can review it on their own time and reach back out when they are ready. I also ask if it is okay to check in with them in a few weeks.

### The Exit Strategy
If they decline all low-friction options and explicitly ask me not to follow up, I thank them for their time and respect their boundary. I leave the door open by saying: "If things change in the future, feel free to reach out. All the best!"

---

## State 4: Cold, Curt, or Directly Disinterested (The "No" State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate has clearly and directly said they are not interested. They may have given a reason or simply said "no." This is a clear boundary that must be respected.

### The Ideal Goal
Exit gracefully while preserving the relationship and generating optionality. I thank them for their time, acknowledge their decision, and ask a single soft referral question: "Do you know anyone in your network who might be a fit?"

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they say no to the referral ask, I simply thank them again and wish them well. No further follow-up.

### The Exit Strategy
The exit itself is the outcome. I do not try to convince them. I leave a positive impression so that if their situation changes, or if they encounter someone in their network who is a fit, they think of me.

---

## State 5: Ghosting or Delayed Response (The Silent State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate has viewed the message or received the notification but has not replied within forty-eight hours. They are not engaged, but they have also not said no. This is a state of passive indifference.

### The Ideal Goal
Re-engage with a single, value-added follow-up that offers new information they did not see before. I do not ask "did you see my message?" I introduce a new data point or benefit. If they reply to this, I transition them to State 1 or 2.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they do not reply to the follow-up, I accept the loss and move on. I do not send a second follow-up because that risks damaging my sender reputation and appearing desperate.

### The Exit Strategy
No explicit exit message is sent. I simply stop engaging. If the candidate replies weeks later, I am happy to re-engage, but I do not chase further.

---

## State 6: Post-Interview Completion (They Came Back to Me)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate has completed the interview and has come back to me, either proactively or in response to my follow-up. This is the most critical moment for attribution. They are curious about the results and open to next steps.

### The Ideal Goal
Secure attribution by sharing my personal Dub referral link and getting them onboarded on my end. I frame this as a service to them: "Let me get you onboarded properly so I can track your results and match you with other roles." I then introduce the referral bonus program.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they are hesitant to click the Dub link immediately, I offer to send the link via a separate message or email for their convenience. I also offer to answer any questions they have about the onboarding process.

### The Exit Strategy
If they complete the interview but refuse to engage with the Dub link entirely, I respect their choice. I offer to keep them in my network for future opportunities and exit gracefully, ensuring they leave with a positive impression.

---

## State 7: Post-Interview Ghosting (They Took the Interview but Didn't Come Back)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate took the interview but did not come back to me, despite the seed I planted. They may have gotten busy, forgotten, or assumed the process was complete.

### The Ideal Goal
Send a single follow-up that re-engages them with a clear next step. I say: "Hey, did you get a chance to complete the interview? Once you do, I'll help you create your agent on my end. This way I can track your results and match you with other roles too. Here is my personal link to get started—it helps me keep you in my pipeline."

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they reply but are still hesitant to click the Dub link, I offer to send the job description link for another role or simply keep them in my network for future opportunities.

### The Exit Strategy
If they do not reply to this follow-up, I accept the loss and move on. I do not send additional follow-ups because the seed was already planted and they chose not to act.

---

## State 8: Highly Enthusiastic and Asking for Immediate Next Steps (The "How Do I Apply?" State)

### Psychological Profile
The candidate is not just interested but is actively driving the conversation forward, asking about the application process or interview timeline. This is the ideal state.

### The Ideal Goal
Facilitate immediate action. I share the direct interview link, outline the exact interview process steps and timeline, and ask for their preferred availability to speak with the hiring team. I get out of their way and let their intrinsic motivation carry the momentum.

### The Minimum Viable Outcome
If they are enthusiastic but need time to prepare before taking the interview, I offer to send the job description link for review and ask when they would like me to follow up.

### The Exit Strategy
There is no exit strategy needed in this state. The goal is to get out of their way and let them move forward.
