# AI Writing Patterns to AVOID (from Wikipedia: Signs of AI Writing)

**Last Updated:** 2026-05-24  
**Reference:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

---

## CRITICAL PATTERNS TO AVOID

### 1. **Repetitive Summary/Conclusion Phrases**
❌ AVOID: "In summary...", "Overall...", "In conclusion...", "To summarize..."
✅ USE: Just move on. Don't signal the ending. Let it end naturally.

### 2. **Rigid Formulaic Structure**
❌ AVOID: Predictable sections like "Challenges", "Future Prospects", "In the future..."
✅ USE: Organic paragraph flow that matches conversation, not templates.

### 3. **Generic Positive Descriptors**
❌ AVOID: "innovative", "cutting-edge", "transformative", "groundbreaking"
✅ USE: Specific details. Real examples. Actual numbers.

### 4. **AI Vocabulary Red Flags**
❌ AVOID these overused words:
- delve, intricate, tapestry, pivotal
- underscore, landscape, foster, testament
- enhance, crucial, dynamic, robust
- intricate, nuanced, multifaceted

✅ USE: Simple, direct language your friends would use.

### 5. **"Rule of Threes" (Triplet Listings)**
❌ AVOID: Listing exactly 3 of everything (bullets, adjectives, examples)
✅ USE: List 2, 4, or 5. Vary the count naturally.

### 6. **Excessive Formatting**
❌ AVOID: Overuse of:
- Bold text (**bold**)
- Bullet lists (especially in narrative sections)
- Title Case In Headers
- EMOJIS 😀
- Curly quotes/"smart quotes"

✅ USE: Minimal formatting. Mostly plain text. One or two strategic bolds max.

### 7. **Negative Parallelisms**
❌ AVOID: "not only... but also...", "neither... nor...", structured contrasts
✅ USE: Simple statements. Direct language.

### 8. **Superficial Analysis**
❌ AVOID: Vague attributions like "some critics argue", "many people believe"
✅ USE: Specific examples. Named people. Real situations. What YOU observed.

### 9. **M-DASHES and N-DASHES**
❌ AVOID: "This is important—here's why" or "This is important – here's why"
✅ USE: Replace with commas or periods. "This is important. Here's why." or "This is important, and here's why."

### 10. **Knowledge Cutoff Language**
❌ AVOID: "As an AI language model...", "I was trained on...", "My knowledge cutoff..."
✅ USE: Just don't mention it. Write naturally.

### 11. **Prompt Refusals in Text**
❌ AVOID: Showing hesitation or AI limitations mid-text
✅ USE: Commit to the narrative. No apologies.

### 12. **Excessive Compliments (Overpositive Tone)**
❌ AVOID: "I'm thrilled to...", "I'm incredibly excited about...", "This is truly remarkable..."
✅ USE: Genuine interest. Measured tone. Human skepticism is okay.

## WHAT TO DO INSTEAD (Cover Letter Specific)

### ✅ Write Like a Human
- **Be specific.** Name projects, people, outcomes. Real numbers.
- **Show your thinking.** Not just what you did, but why you cared.
- **Use varied sentence length.** Short. Medium. Long. Mix it up.
- **Be conversational.** Like you're talking to a friend, not a corporate form.
- **Show personality.** Your voice should come through.

### ✅ Structure
- Opening: Why THIS company, THIS role (specific, not generic)
- Middle: 2-3 real examples of YOUR impact (not templated achievements)
- Closing: What excites you about the work (not about the company prestige)
- Length: ~250-350 words. Tight. No fluff.

### ✅ Tone
- Confident but humble
- Curious, not desperate
- Specific, not flowery
- Direct, not meandering

### ✅ Content Strategy
- **Show, don't tell.** Instead of "I'm a great problem-solver", show a problem you solved and how.
- **Talk about things NOT on resume.** Why you cared about compliance. What drew you to payments. Personal motivation.
- **Present as belonging.** "Here's why I'm built for this" not "Please pick me."
- **Mention obstacles/learnings.** Humans fail and learn. Show that.

## ANTI-PATTERNS CHECKLIST

Before sending, check:
- [ ] No "In summary" or "Overall" phrases
- [ ] No lists in narrative sections (max 1 bulleted list in whole letter)
- [ ] No m-dashes or n-dashes (replaced with commas/periods)
- [ ] No generic AI vocab (delve, intricate, tapestry, etc.)
- [ ] No "rule of threes" (varied list lengths)
- [ ] Specific examples, not vague statements
- [ ] Conversational tone (read aloud—does it sound human?)
- [ ] Shows personality
- [ ] Mentions things NOT on resume
- [ ] Explains motivation/inspiration/why this matters
